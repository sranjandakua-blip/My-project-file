# Triple Hook Method

A hook works when the viewer, within the first second or two:
1. **buys into the context**: "this is about something I care about",
2. **leans in**: "I'm curious where this goes",
3. **gets snapped back**: "wait, that's not what I expected" (now they have to stay).

Context + Lean = the curiosity loop. The Snapback is what makes leaving feel impossible.

## Step 1: Context (immediately)

Viewers only watch what they already have a stake in, so **the first line has to say what the video is about.**

- Put the topic in the **first words** of sentence one. Pushing it even to the back half of the first sentence sharply raises the skip rate.
- Set it up on as many channels as you can, ideally all three:
  - **Audio**: say the topic out loud.
  - **Visual**: show the subject (product, place, result, screen).
  - **Text**: on-screen title or caption that names the topic.
- Pick a topic the target viewer already cares about. If it is outside their interest, no hook will save it.

❌ "So I was sitting at my desk the other day and something hit me about content…"
✅ "Your Instagram hook is why nobody watches past second two."

## Step 2: The Lean (curiosity in one direction)

Get the viewer **curious in a specific direction**, so they actively want more instead of opting out. Use at least one lever:

| Lever | Pattern | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Proof / stat** | A number or result that backs up the claim that's coming | "…we tested 1,200 hooks to find this." |
| **Pain point** | Name a specific frustration they have right now | "…if your Reels die at 200 views…" |
| **Desired benefit** | Name the outcome they want | "…how to get 10x the watch time on the same video." |
| **Interesting / shocking / unexpected** | A surprising fact, visual or claim | "…the hook the biggest creators use isn't a question." |

**Context-Lean check:** read the first one or two sentences **on their own**.
- Is the context immediately clear? ✓
- Is there something that makes them curious, so they want more? ✓

If either answer is no, rewrite before moving on.

## Step 3: Contrarian Snapback (the money step)

To catch a fish, you set the hook while it swims one way, then **yank the line the other way.** Hooks work the same way: once the viewer leans one way, **say something that pulls them off that path** onto another one.

Snapback patterns:
- **Contradict the obvious conclusion**: "…but that's not why it works."
- **Flip the expected cause**: "…and it has nothing to do with [the thing they assume]."
- **Reverse the stakes**: "…which is exactly why it's about to fail."
- **"Everyone thinks X. It's actually Y."**
- **Plot twist on the setup**: "…and the guy who built it almost got fired for it."

Source example: Context = "The Vegas Sphere is the biggest screen ever built." Lean = something impressive about it. Snapback = a turn that sends the story somewhere the viewer didn't expect (the real story isn't the screen).

The snapback has to be **true and paid off** in the body. A snapback that the video never delivers on is clickbait, and retention drops off a cliff.

## How to use this skill

### When asked to WRITE hooks
1. Pin down the **topic**, the **target viewer** and the **payoff** (what the body actually delivers). If the topic or payoff is missing, ask once.
2. Draft **5 hook options**, each built with a **different Lean lever** and **Snapback pattern**.
3. For each option, output:

```
#N — <lever> + <snapback pattern>
🎙 Audio:   "<spoken line(s), 1–3 sentences, ≤ ~5 s / ~15 words before the snapback>"
👁 Visual:  <what's on screen in frame 1>
🔤 Text:    <on-screen title, ≤ 6 words>
Context ✓  Lean ✓  Snapback ✓  — <one-line why it works>
```

4. Say which **one you'd pick** and why.

### When asked to FIX or REVIEW a hook
Score it on the three steps (✓ / ⚠️ / ✗), name the failure, then give a rewrite:
- **Context late or missing?** Move the topic word to the start. Add a matching visual and text.
- **No lean?** Add a stat, pain, benefit or shock.
- **No snapback?** Add a turn that contradicts what they now expect.
- **Snapback not paid off?** Change the snapback or the body so they match.

### Rules of thumb
- Spoken hook: **1–3 short sentences**; the first words carry the topic.
- Audio, visual and on-screen text should **agree**, with all three saying the topic.
- Cut warm-ups ("Hey guys", "So…", "In this video…") before the context line.
- Make it specific: numbers, names and concrete nouns beat abstractions.
- Don't use the identical snapback twice in a row for the same audience; it stops feeling contrarian.

## Worked example

Topic: meal prep for busy people. Payoff: a 3-ingredient system.

```
#1 — Pain + flip the expected cause
🎙 Audio:   "Meal prep fails for busy people every single Sunday. And it's not because you're lazy — it's because you're cooking too many recipes."
👁 Visual:  Fridge full of untouched prep containers
🔤 Text:    Why your meal prep fails
Context ✓ (meal prep, first two words)  Lean ✓ (pain: it fails)  Snapback ✓ (not laziness → too many recipes)
```
