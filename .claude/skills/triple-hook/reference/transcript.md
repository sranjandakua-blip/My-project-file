**[00:10] Step 1: The Context**
All right, step one is the context. And this is the basic step, but so many people still get this wrong. Viewers only watch content where they have a vested interest in the topic. So the first line of your hook must establish context on what the video is gonna be about. You can say it in the audio ("Seven figure content toolkit"), show it in the visuals, write it in the text or caption. Ideally all three. But the context must be established immediately. We have data that shows even delaying the context to the back half of the first sentence increases the skip rate dramatically. So priority number one is to set immediate context as soon as possible.

**[00:44] Step 2: The Lean**
Now step two in the triple hook is where most people mess up. This is called the lean. What you're trying to do is get someone curious in a certain direction. You wanna make them lean in on the context to actively want to hear more. Now you can make this lean-in happen in a variety of ways. You can give some proof or stat validating the incoming claim, reference a specific pain point they have or a benefit they want, or share something interesting, shocking or unexpected. But regardless of what you pick, the viewer must lean in instead of opt out.

So when you read back that first one to two sentences just on its own: is the context immediately clear, and is there some curiosity inducement that makes the viewer want to lean in for more? It's called the context lean. This is what opens the curiosity loop [you] hear everybody talk about.

**[01:27] Step 3: The Contrarian Snapback**
Now step three in the triple hook is really where the money's made. Like 1% of people know how to do this. I call it the contrarian snapback. To catch a fish, you gotta get the hook in its mouth swimming one way and then yank at the line going another direction. Not to be morbid, but storytelling works the exact same way. The goal is to say something in the contrarian snapback that pulls the viewer away from the direction they were leaning, essentially snapping [them] back down another path.
